03 · What fieldwork revealed

Much of the friction lived outside the tools

On ride-alongs and in contextual interviews, I watched how engineers actually get through a service day.

Before fieldwork, I expected friction from

DocumentationSoftware accessEquipment

These still mattered.

On ride-alongs, I saw it also come from

Travel logisticsSite requirementsCommunication delaysCoordination with others

These shaped the day just as much.

How I watched

InterruptionsWorkaroundsTransitionsCoordination with staff

The question I carried: what made this adaptation necessary?

What changed in my thinking

These factors become routine, so they rarely come up in an interview. Watching the work moved my focus from the task to the conditions around it.

04 · The system around the engineer

The repair is one part of a larger system

Each factor below came up in fieldwork. The questions are what I would study next; none of them were measured.

Getting there

Observed: travel logistics were a real source of friction in the day.

Next question: how does travel affect time on site and the order of tasks?

The site and equipment

Observed: site requirements shaped the work as much as the equipment did.

Next question: what do engineers need to know before they arrive?

Tools and information

Observed: documentation and software access still mattered, as expected.

Next question: where is information hardest to reach mid-task, and what interrupts it?

People and handoffs

Observed: coordination with lab staff and communication delays shaped the pace of work.

Next question: how do engineers know where others are in a handoff?

06 · What I learned and what’s next

What this project taught me about research

Lesson 01

Context reveals the unspoken

Travel, site rules and waiting on others are easy to leave out of an interview, but hard to miss on a ride-along.

Lesson 02

Problems live in the system

The friction came from how people, sites, tools and schedules interact. I felt some of it myself while coordinating the visits.

Lesson 03

Separate observation from interpretation

In synthesis, I’m holding off on calling something a pattern until it shows up across engineers.

Where it stands

Coordination and fieldwork are complete. I’m leading synthesis, the journey map and the storyboard, which are in progress. Recommendations and final outcomes haven’t been established yet.

Questions for a next phase

How do interruptions affect resuming work? Where is information hardest to reach mid-task? How visible is the status of a handoff? How does workload build across a day? None were measured here.
