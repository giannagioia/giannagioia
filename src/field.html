Field service ethnography · Client research

Understanding the work behind the work

For a confidential client, our team studied how field service engineers at biotech labs get their work done. Over seven ride-alongs and contextual interviews, we saw that travel, site requirements and coordination with lab staff shaped the day as much as their tools did. I coordinated the fieldwork, took part in it, and am leading synthesis.

01 · Why observe the work

Watching the work surfaced what interviews alone might miss

Much of this work happens on the road and on site. Engineers adapt to conditions so routinely that they may not think to mention them in an interview, so we went along for the day.

Before fieldwork, I expected friction from

DocumentationSoftware accessEquipment

These still mattered.

On ride-alongs, I saw it also come from

Travel logisticsSite requirementsCommunication delaysCoordination with others

These shaped the day just as much.

What I watched for

InterruptionsWorkaroundsTransitionsCoordination with staff

The question I carried: what made this adaptation necessary?

What changed in my thinking

Observing the work helped me identify factors I might otherwise have missed, and moved my focus from the task itself to the conditions around it.

05 · Where it stands and what I learned

What this project taught me about field research

Lesson 01

Context reveals the unspoken

Travel, site rules and waiting on others are easy to leave out of an interview, but hard to miss on a ride-along.

Lesson 02

Problems live in the system

The friction came from how people, sites, tools and schedules interact. I felt some of it myself while coordinating the visits.

Lesson 03

Separate observation from interpretation

In synthesis, I’m holding off on calling something a pattern until it shows up across engineers.

Where it stands

Coordination and fieldwork are complete. I’m leading synthesis, the journey map and the storyboard, all still in progress. Recommendations and final outcomes haven’t been established yet.

Questions for a next phase

How do interruptions affect resuming work? Where is information hardest to reach mid-task? How visible is the status of a handoff? How does workload build across a day? None were measured here.
