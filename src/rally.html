People didn’t lack motivation. They lost the structure around movement. Rally is the strategy that followed.

UX research & product strategy · Gianna Gioia

At a glance · What this case shows: translating research into product strategy

Research reframed the product from motivating people to rebuilding structure

My role

Researcher and designer. I framed the question, ran the research, set the product strategy, then designed and coded the prototype.

Evidence

Literature review, athletic trainer interviews, 7 peer interviews, a 16-app competitive analysis and exploratory testing with 3 people.

Strategy outcome

Three product bets, each traced to evidence. Scope cut from five tabs to three. A coded prototype at lets-rally.vercel.app, revised after testing.

What shaped behavior

People already believed movement mattered. It slipped when routines changed, spaces felt crowded or accountability disappeared.

01 · Where it started

I started with an assumption and tested it before building

As a college athlete, movement came built in: practices, teammates, a set routine. In a 9-to-5, that structure disappeared. My assumption: losing the team is the hard part.

The question I tested instead

When the structure around movement disappears, what helps people keep moving?

Why it mattered for strategy

Building from my own story would have meant designing for former athletes. Testing it first let the evidence decide who Rally was for.

04 · Finding the strategic position

Sixteen apps each solved part of the problem

Four apps per category, compared on features, UX, reviews and what was missing.

Gamification

Duolingo, Words With Friends, Strava, Finch

Borrowed: streaks and visible progress.

Rejected: competition that intimidates beginners.

Community

Reddit, Pinterest, Strava, Nike Run Club

Borrowed: shared experience and belonging.

Rejected: inspiration that rarely turns into action.

Movement & fitness

Apple Fitness, Fitbit, Nike Run Club, MoveSpring

Borrowed: goals that give structure.

Rejected: workouts prioritized over everyday movement.

Habit formation

Finch, Headspace, Calm, Fabulous

Borrowed: check-ins and positive reinforcement.

Rejected: support with no accountability.

Rally’s position

None of the 16 combined motivation without pressure, everyday movement and small-group accountability. That gap is where I placed Rally: an opportunity to test, not proof that combining them works.

09 · Reflection

Supporting behavior change means fitting into people’s lives, not asking them to build new ones

I assumed

Losing a team is the hard part

Based on my own move from athlete to a 9-to-5.

Research showed

Movement lives in everyday routines

It slipped when structure changed, not motivation.

So the strategy became

Rebuild structure, for anyone in transition

Small Rallys, small groups, alongside the gym.

Bets still to prove

Do Huddles and weekly goals keep people moving over weeks? Do these patterns hold beyond seven people?

How I’d test them

Two-week diary study: do Huddles sustain movement?
Broader interviews: do patterns hold beyond my circle?
Second usability round: does onboarding close the gap?
