01 · Project overview

Evaluating small-business software

Working with another researcher and our Design Research Director, I evaluated software for small-business owners: recruiting, the moderation guide, moderated sessions and synthesis. Five sessions in, the same issues kept recurring, so I chose to bring initial findings to the client early rather than wait for all ten participants.

02 · Study planning

Preparing the study

Recruiting

Recruiting participants who reflected the study’s target audience

I wrote the recruiting script and reviewed participants against the client’s criteria: small-business ownership or decision-making responsibility, across a range of revenue levels, locations and industries.

Moderation guide

Designing the study to test understanding and usability

I developed the moderation guide, including tasks, prompts and follow-up questions.

Starting with onboarding let us see whether participants understood the product’s purpose and value, not only whether they could use it.

Session structure

1. Onboarding

Do participants understand what the product is for?

2. Scenario-based task

Can they apply what they learned in a realistic task?

3. Interview

How do they describe its value, ease of use and clarity?

03 · Moderation

Watching what people do, then asking why

In the sessions

I moderated sessions and conducted participant interviews, and observed my teammate’s sessions.

A participant’s comment

“It’s dark, it’s hard to read. Depressing, kinda.”

This made me consider how the interface’s visual tone could shape participants’ perceptions of the product, beyond whether they could complete a task.

When participants struggled

What happened

Several participants became frustrated during tasks.

What I did

I asked what they expected to happen and what was getting in the way, and used their explanations to understand the difficulty.

What I learned

Frustration signaled that something wasn’t working as expected. Follow-up questions helped us understand why.

04 · Analysis and synthesis

What emerged across the first five sessions

After each session, the three of us compared notes. By participants four and five, these themes were recurring. Early patterns, not conclusions. Initial · 5 of 10 participants

01 · Value

Purpose wasn’t clear

Participants were unsure what it did or what they’d get from it.

02 · Tone

Game elements felt off

XP, coins and streaks felt cartoony to some participants for a business tool.

03 · Navigation

No clear starting point

Participants asked which tool to use, and in what order.

04 · Effort

Effort before payoff

Participants gave a lot of information before knowing what they’d get back.

05 · Trust

Security felt unclear

Some participants were unsure the product was secure or transparent.

The strongest pattern, from evidence to question

Observed

Several participants finished onboarding and the scenario task while still unsure what the product was for or what they’d get from it.

Interpretation

Completing a task didn’t mean understanding the product. Its value wasn’t landing during onboarding.

Why it matters

The client asked whether owners see value. Task success alone would have overstated it.

Question for the client

How might onboarding show what owners get back before asking for their business details?

Why share now

By sessions four and five the same issues were recurring, and they were about whether people understood the product at all, not small slips in a task.

Why not wait

Five more sessions on the same version would likely re-confirm those issues. Sharing at the halfway point let the client consider changes while the remaining sessions could still show their effect.

Keeping the study sound

Findings went over as early patterns from 5 of 10 participants. The study continues as planned, and later results will be read against the version each participant used.

Where it stands: the client received initial findings and recommendations and is considering revisions. Nothing has been changed or validated yet.

06 · Reflection and next steps

What this project taught me

As my first usability testing project, this changed how I think about the work. It isn’t just identifying issues and reporting them at the end. It’s recognizing patterns across participants, understanding the reasons behind their behavior, and deciding when the evidence is useful enough to inform a decision.

Sharing initial findings before the study was complete showed me how research can support product decisions while there is still time to act.
